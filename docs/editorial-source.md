# Which Famous Writer Are You

W4GZ Your writing style

100 questions with editorial answer mappings

Choose the answer closest to the writing you would like to create. The result suggests literary affinities with a writer’s work; it does not establish that you share the writer’s private personality, beliefs or life history.

## How to use this edition

The short quiz uses questions 1 to 25. The extended quiz uses questions 1 to 50. Questions 51 to 60 form an additional round, and questions 61 to 100 form a further 40-question bank. The complete quiz uses all 100 questions. Each question has four answers. The gray writer names and reference notes are for editors and should be hidden from quiz participants. Randomize answer order while keeping each answer linked to its mapping.

The mappings identify specific affinities, not exclusive descriptions of an author’s entire career. A writer may suit more than one answer, and an unlisted writer should not be treated as the opposite of that answer. The reference index names the works behind the associations.

### Scoring

Give one match to each writer listed under the selected answer. For each writer, divide their matches by the number of administered questions in which they appear in at least one option. Use that proportion to rank affinities and show the three closest results, together with the match count and opportunity count. Count each writer at most once per question. Break equal proportions by higher match count, then report any remaining tie. Do not label these scores as personality probabilities.

These selective mappings change the original scoring coverage. Treat the ranking as an editorial starting point: before release, pilot the selected quiz length, inspect unequal opportunities and unstable results, and adjust coverage. A writer with very few opportunities should not be presented as a confident winner.

## Coverage balancing for questions 1 to 25

### Historical qualifications

Homer’s identity and biography remain uncertain; the mappings concern the Iliad and Odyssey traditionally attributed to Homer. Much of Sappho’s work survives in fragments, so gaps in survival do not establish how little she originally wrote. Cao Xueqin is associated with the first 80 chapters of Dream of the Red Chamber; the later continuation should not be treated as secure evidence of his intended ending. Translation can change apparent sentence length, rhythm and diction, especially across poetic traditions.
